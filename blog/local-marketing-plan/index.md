# A practical marketing plan for an Okanagan service business

A useful marketing plan begins with a specific service, a clear audience and a next step. Decide what you want to sell more of, where you can deliver it and what makes it a good fit for that customer. That gives your website, search content and campaigns something concrete to explain.

## Write down why a customer chooses you.

Look at recent jobs and conversations. What prompted someone to contact you? What did they worry about? What evidence helped them decide? Use those answers to sharpen your positioning. Replace broad claims about quality with details you can show: a relevant project, a clear process, specialist experience or the way you handle a particular job.

## Make local discovery consistent.

Review the service information on your website and Google Business Profile together. Check contact details, hours, service areas and the route to an enquiry. Describe the areas you actually serve, including the practical limits. Useful local content should help a customer understand the work; publishing near-identical pages for every town does not create a better buying experience.

## Give content a role in the sale.

Choose topics from questions people ask before buying. A case study can demonstrate the finished work. A service page can explain the scope. An article can help someone prepare for an estimate. Connect each piece to the relevant service and contact route. A smaller collection of useful, maintained pages is easier to trust than a large collection of vague articles.

## Build a campaign people can act on.

For a seasonal offer or new service, agree the audience, message, destination and action. Prepare the landing page and supporting creative together so the promise stays consistent. Decide who answers enquiries and how quickly they can respond before driving more traffic to the offer. Media spend, production costs and management responsibilities should be explicit in the scope.

## Separate attention from useful demand.

Visits and impressions tell you that something was seen. Calls, qualified enquiries and completed bookings tell you more about commercial value. Review these separately, using the data you can actually collect. Compare equivalent periods and account for seasonal changes before attributing a change to a campaign.

You may need clearer positioning before a campaign, better service pages before more traffic, or stronger proposals once leads arrive.
