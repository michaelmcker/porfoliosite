## 1. Compare the actual website scope

A website quote should name the pages, audiences and actions it covers. A small service site with an enquiry form is a different project from an online shop or a booking system with staff schedules and payments.

What changes the scope of a website

| Build | Main work | Questions to settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focused service website | Service pages, proof and enquiry route | Who writes the content and handles enquiries? |
| Booking website | Availability, services, confirmations and integrations | Is the booking system already chosen? |
| Online store | Product setup, checkout and orders | Who supplies product data and manages fulfilment? |

For a CAD $3,000–$4,000 planning budget, my recommendation is to prioritise the core service website and quote substantial integrations separately. Michael McKerracher’s web design is custom-priced against an agreed scope, rather than a universal fixed package.

## 2. Find out who writes and supplies the content

Website content is real project work. A quote should specify whether the designer interviews you, plans the page structure, writes the copy, prepares FAQs and turns past projects into useful evidence.

If you supply the words and images, agree the format and delivery date. If you need them created, price that work at the start. “Five pages” might mean five blank layouts waiting for your content or five finished pages with research, writing and approved imagery. Those are not equivalent deliverables.

Separate brand photography and video from image selection where appropriate. Original work can make a website more persuasive, but the production requirements should be visible in the quote.

## 3. Understand what custom design includes

A website design quote should explain what is being designed and how feedback works. Ask whether you are choosing an existing theme, adapting a design system or commissioning a visual direction built around the business.

Look beyond the homepage. Request examples of service pages, forms and mobile layouts. A useful design system covers headings, spacing, colours, buttons, images and repeatable content patterns, so the website can grow consistently.

Ask how many review stages are included and what counts as a scope change. Agree who signs off the direction before the full build. That reduces expensive revisions caused by making foundational decisions too late.

## 4. Separate search setup from ongoing SEO

A website quote should identify the search work included at launch. Useful foundations include page planning, titles, headings, internal links, crawlability, sitemap setup and redirects where existing URLs change.

Ongoing SEO is a separate activity: finding new content opportunities, improving existing pages, maintaining business information and measuring the results. A promise of an “SEO website” needs to be translated into specific tasks.

Ask which accounts you will control and how results will be reported. Search Console impressions, website enquiries and booked work are different measures. A report should explain the connection without treating every visitor as a prospective customer.

## 5. Specify the booking and business-system work

A website integration costs more than adding a button when it needs to pass data between systems. Identify which booking tool, CRM, payment system or email platform is involved, and who supplies access.

Test what happens after the user clicks. Does the correct service carry through? Is the booking confirmed? Where does the enquiry appear? Who handles errors? A simple link to an existing booking platform has a different scope from custom scheduling or automated follow-up.

List recurring software charges separately from the developer’s work. That lets you compare the total cost of the arrangement rather than only the launch invoice.

## 6. Put ownership and handover in writing

A website quote should explain who controls the domain, hosting, content, design files and platform account. Ask what can be exported and what happens if you change providers.

Decide whether you want to edit the site yourself, ask for occasional changes or buy ongoing support. Training should cover the work you will actually do. A handover can include account access, editing instructions, form routing, renewal dates and a record of important integrations.

When comparing a monthly website offer with an upfront build, ask about minimum terms, cancellation, buyout and migration. The monthly number alone does not explain what you own.

## 7. Compare the continuing cost, not just launch day

A website’s continuing costs can include domain renewal, hosting or platform fees, maintenance, content changes and optional marketing. Request an annual view of these costs alongside the initial project price.

## Questions, answered.

### Is CAD $3,000–$4,000 enough for my website?

It can be a sensible planning budget for a focused service website when the scope is controlled. The exact quote depends on content, design, page count and integrations. Complex booking, ecommerce or ongoing marketing should be scoped explicitly.

### Are hosting and SEO included in a website build?

Only if the quote says so. Ask for hosting, platform fees, maintenance, launch search setup and ongoing SEO to be listed separately. That makes both the first-year cost and future commitments easier to understand.

### How can I reduce cost without weakening the website?

Prioritise the services and pages that help customers choose, supply usable project material promptly and keep integrations simple at launch. Reduce unnecessary scope before cutting useful content, mobile usability or a reliable enquiry route.
