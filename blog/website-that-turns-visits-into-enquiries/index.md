# What makes a local business website worth enquiring through?

A local business website needs to explain what you do, where you work, why someone should trust you and how to take the next step. Beautiful design helps make the business memorable. Useful information helps someone decide whether to call. The strongest brief brings those two requirements together from the beginning.

## Match the experience to the business.

An accommodation website needs to help a guest imagine the stay. The Okanagan Treehouse preview uses property imagery and distinct stories for each accommodation. A school or parish has a different task: communicate its character while making practical information easy to find. The RCCV design puts both into the same experience. A template should not decide what matters most to your customer.

## Build service pages around real questions.

For a local service business, describe the work in enough detail that someone can judge the fit. Explain the service area, typical situations, information needed for a quote and anything that affects the scope. Use photographs of real work and relevant examples. A page that only repeats a service name and city has little to help a prospective customer decide.

## Put proof beside the claim.

Show the project when discussing design. Link the case study when discussing a business result. In the Cool Runnings case study, the sales increase is attributed to the client report, with separate performance information available for context. Different evidence answers different questions: a finished design demonstrates craft; an enquiry report helps evaluate the customer journey.

## Check the complete mobile journey.

Read the page on a phone, tap the contact action and submit the form. Check that labels are visible, fields are usable and the confirmation explains what happens next. Test booking links and phone links. A prominent button is only useful if the process behind it works and someone receives the enquiry.

## Plan search and measurement before launch.

Agree the pages and their purpose before writing titles or metadata. Keep important information in readable HTML, link related pages and include canonical pages in a sitemap. Preserve valuable URLs or map redirects during a redesign. Define useful conversion events separately from general visits, then use the available data to review what people actually do.

## Bring a business brief, not just a page count.

Tell me who you need to reach, what you want them to do and what is failing on the current site. Bring your existing brand material, photographs and examples of customer questions.
