# Website visitors but no enquiries? 7 checks for local businesses

When a website gets traffic but few enquiries, first check who is visiting and whether the contact route actually works. Then examine the offer, proof, service information and follow-up. More visitors will only help when the website gives suitable customers a clear reason and a reliable way to get in touch.

## 1. Check whether the visitors want what you sell

A local business website needs relevant visitors, not just a rising session count. Review the search queries and landing pages that bring people in. A person researching a DIY repair may need different information from someone ready to hire an appliance technician.

Group enquiries by requested service and location. If many are outside your coverage or for work you do not provide, clarify those details in the page title, opening copy and form. An Okanagan company serving scheduled work should not attract emergency requests it cannot fulfil through an overbroad promise.

## 2. Make the service understandable in the first screen

A website visitor should be able to identify the business, service and next step without interpreting vague slogans. Read the headline and first paragraph aloud to someone unfamiliar with the company. Ask what they think you do and how they would contact you.

If the answer is unclear, rewrite the introduction before adding more sections. Lead with the customer’s problem and the service you provide. Use technical detail deeper in the page where it helps someone make a decision, rather than making it the entry requirement.

## 3. Answer the questions blocking the enquiry

A service website can lose a suitable enquiry because one practical question remains unanswered. Coverage, estimate process, availability, minimum project size and what is included can all affect whether a visitor gets in touch.

Ask the person answering calls which questions come up repeatedly. Put the useful answers on the relevant service page. For a home builder, explain the kinds of projects you take and how the initial meeting works. For a clinic, explain how a new patient requests an appointment and where they should send private information.

## 4. Replace broad claims with relevant evidence

A website needs evidence that fits the purchase. Photographs of finished work, a clear process and an attributed customer comment can help explain why a business is a good choice. Generic praise without context gives the visitor less to evaluate.

Place evidence near the point where the visitor is deciding, rather than relying on a separate gallery they may never open.

## 5. Test the form, phone link and confirmation

A website can look finished while losing real requests. Submit the form using a test clearly marked as such, check the notification arrives and verify the confirmation describes the next step. Test on a phone as well as a desktop.

What to check before buying more traffic

| Symptom | First check |
| --- | --- |
| Form starts but few completions | Required fields, errors and mobile entry |
| Submissions appear in analytics but not the inbox | Form delivery, spam filtering and notification settings |
| Many phone taps but few known calls | Correct number and separate call measurement |
| Bookings stop at an external tool | Availability, service selection and checkout handoff |

Do not use button taps alone as a count of completed enquiries. The distinction matters when deciding whether to change the website or the response process.

## 6. Find out what happens after someone contacts you

A website enquiry needs an operational owner. Review how quickly requests are noticed, whether the response answers the question and whether suitable prospects receive follow-up.

Use a simple lead record with the service, location, source, qualification and booking outcome. A small business can start with a shared spreadsheet. More involved teams may benefit from routing, reminders or a CRM workflow.

If a confirmation promises a callback, make sure someone can deliver it. Automation can acknowledge receipt and organise the request, while a person handles judgement, scope and the customer conversation.

## 7. Change one important problem and review the result

A [website improvement](https://michaelmck.site/web-design/)
 should address the clearest observed problem first. If customers cannot understand the service, fix the copy. If the form is broken, repair it. If unsuitable traffic dominates, revisit the search or advertising approach.

Record what changed and when, then review comparable periods with enough enquiries to be meaningful. Seasonality and changes to advertising can affect the result. For low-volume businesses, direct feedback and careful enquiry records may be more useful than declaring a conversion-rate winner after a few visits.

## Questions, answered.

### Should I redesign the whole website if it gets no leads?

Not automatically. First check traffic relevance, service clarity and whether forms and phone links work. A focused repair may solve the immediate problem; a rebuild makes sense when the structure or platform prevents useful improvements. Follow the main customer task on a phone and submit a test enquiry before committing to a redesign. Check whether the page describes the service the visitor searched for and whether the team receives the request. Fixing a specific failure gives you a clearer basis for deciding whether broader work is needed.

### What is a good conversion rate for a local website?

There is no single rate that applies to every local business. Define the conversion first, then compare similar traffic and periods. A completed booking, a contact form and a phone-button tap should not be treated as the same outcome. Separate suitable enquiries from spam, irrelevant requests and duplicate contacts, then follow those leads to accepted work. Compare like-for-like periods and traffic sources. A low-volume local site can show large percentage swings from a single enquiry, so read the individual requests as well as the aggregate rate.

### How do I know whether the website or my ads are the problem?

Compare the search terms or campaign audience with the landing page and actual enquiries. Irrelevant visitors point toward targeting or message problems. Suitable visitors who cannot complete the next step point toward the page, form or booking flow. Compare the search or ad intent with the landing page, then check where suitable visitors leave the journey. If requests arrive but do not become jobs, review qualification and follow-up as well. Keep one clear record of the changes you make so the next review can identify what was actually implemented.

### How do I tell whether a form is silently losing enquiries?

Submit a clearly identified test, check the confirmation and verify its arrival in the destination inbox or lead system. Test the mobile journey and any error state the provider supports. A success message on the website is not enough if nobody receives the request or staff cannot identify what service was requested.

### Should I show prices to improve enquiry quality?

Show useful pricing context when you can state it accurately: a starting scope, assessment fee or explanation of how quotes are prepared. Describe what changes the price and what happens next. The goal is to help suitable customers judge fit, rather than publish a number that excludes essential work or creates false expectations.
