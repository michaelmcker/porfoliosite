7 ways to help customers find your business on Google and ChatGPT

Help Google and ChatGPT understand your business by publishing clear, accessible service information, original proof and accurate local details. Allow the relevant search crawlers to reach public content, make important answers easy to extract and measure the enquiries that follow. These are discoverability foundations, not a guarantee of a recommendation.

1. Let search systems reach the useful pages

A business website must be accessible before its content can be considered for search. Check that important pages return successfully, are linked from other public pages and are not accidentally marked noindex or blocked by access controls.

OpenAI’s publisher guidance says to allow OAI-SearchBot for content to be included in ChatGPT search summaries and snippets. This is a separate control from GPTBot, which relates to potential model training. Ask your developer to inspect robots.txt and any firewall rules instead of assuming that a public-looking page is reachable by every crawler.

For Google, use Search Console to inspect the actual URL and review indexing information. A sitemap submission helps discovery; it does not prove that a page has entered the index.

2. Say exactly what the business does

A business website should name the business, explain its services and identify its real location or coverage. Keep those details consistent across the website and the public profiles the business controls.

The same principle applies to an electrician, clinic or hotel: say who you help, what you provide and where you provide it.

Use structured data to describe information already visible on the page. Do not add offices, reviews, credentials or services the business does not actually have.

3. Put direct answers where people ask the questions

A service page should answer its main question near the top, then explain the details. Use descriptive headings, short self-contained sections, comparison tables where appropriate and concise FAQs for genuine follow-up questions.

For example, a repair page can explain which appliances are serviced, the coverage area, what information helps with the booking and how the visit is arranged. A hotel page should answer different questions about rooms, location, amenities and reservations.

Keep important answers as readable text. A screenshot can illustrate the work, but the service description should not exist only inside an image. The industry website collection shows how the information needs change by business type.

4. Add evidence that belongs to your business

Original business evidence gives readers something useful to evaluate and sources something concrete to reference. Publish project explanations, actual photographs, approved customer comments and measured results with their meaning intact.

Cool Runnings’ 30% increase refers to qualified bookings, not website traffic or revenue. ABC Appliance’s work supports a discussion of repair content and search visibility without an invented percentage. Preserving those distinctions makes the material more useful than a generic claim about growth.

A project story can answer what the problem was, what changed and what the owner learned. Give it a stable page and link to it from the relevant service rather than repeating an unexplained statistic across the site.

5. Keep local discovery information accurate

An Okanagan business should keep its Google Business Profile, website and other relevant listings accurate. Google identifies relevance, distance and prominence as the main local-ranking factors. Complete information helps describe the business; it cannot remove geographic distance.

Keep current services, hours, contact information and the correct location or service area. Add useful local pages where the details differ. A Vernon clinic’s arrival instructions and a West Kelowna contractor’s coverage information are different kinds of local value.

Choose directories and industry organisations that genuinely fit the business. A real profile or relevant project mention is more valuable to a customer than an unsupported claim that everyone recommends the company.

6. Get the technical basics right before adding extras

Google says its usual SEO best practices remain relevant to AI Overviews and AI Mode, with no additional special requirements. Start with useful content, accessible pages, good internal links and accurate structured data.

Markdown versions and an llms.txt file can make content convenient for some tools to consume. Treat them as additional formats, not a replacement for the public HTML website or a promised route into AI answers. FAQ markup should describe the visible questions and answers; it is not an endorsement.

A website launch checklist should also include mobile usability, image size, redirects, canonical URLs and contact testing. These are practical foundations that help the website work for people as well as automated systems.

7. Track visibility and business outcomes separately

A business should measure discovery, visits, enquiries and booked work as different stages. Search Console reports Google search performance. Analytics can show referred visits, and the enquiry process can identify which conversations became useful work.

OpenAI says ChatGPT search referral links include a utm_source=chatgpt.com parameter. Keep that information available in reporting, then look at the resulting enquiries. A single answer where your business appears is a snapshot, not a stable ranking position.

Use the data to decide what to improve: missing service information, weak evidence, confusing booking steps or an unanswered local question. That is a more useful programme than publishing more pages simply to increase the count.

Questions, answered.

Can you guarantee my business will be recommended by ChatGPT?

No. Michael McKerracher can improve the website’s accessibility, content, structure and evidence, but answer systems decide what to retrieve and recommend. The practical goal is accurate discovery and useful customer enquiries.

Do I need a separate AI version of my website?

A separate website is not required. Keep the main site complete, readable and crawlable. Optional Markdown and discovery files can offer additional access, while the public HTML pages remain the primary customer experience.

Should I publish a new page for every nearby town?

Only when each page can help someone with distinct, accurate information. Real coverage, local projects, arrival instructions or service differences can justify a page. Repeating the same copy with a different place name adds little value.
