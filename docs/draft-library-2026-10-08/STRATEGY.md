# The practical authority on small-business websites

The editorial direction is straightforward: help an owner decide what their website should do, how it should look, which tools suit the business and what is worth paying for. Michael's Okanagan work supplies the proof and perspective. The useful parts should also make sense to a business owner outside the region.

This library supports the existing web-design service and its industry pages. It does not replace them with another set of sales pages. The resources answer a buying or operating question; a contextual link leads to the service when someone needs implementation.

## What the library covers

- **Industry websites:** different customer decisions for plumbers, electricians, accountants, lawyers, hotels, landscapers, wineries, vacation rentals, dentists, physiotherapists, restaurants, property managers, retailers, roofers, medical practices, car dealerships, financial advisors, medical spas, appliance repair, schools and charities.
- **Platforms:** Framer, Webflow, WordPress, Shopify, WooCommerce, custom builds, self-hosting and Sanity. Compare editing, content structure, integrations, maintenance, full costs and the exit plan. A CMS, a site builder and hosting are different decisions.
- **Design:** visual hierarchy, typography, photography, motion, service homepages, accommodation storytelling, templates versus custom work and accessibility.
- **Conversion and discovery:** contact paths, useful intake, follow-up, location pages, qualified enquiries, ads readiness and Google/AI search.
- **Buying and ownership:** briefs, quotes, subscriptions, handover, training, measurement and the next annual plan.
- **Marketing and operations:** brand deliverables, donor films, customer stories, social/video roles and practical AI skills/workflows.

## What makes this Michael's library

Use his actual decisions and work: ABC Appliance's service and enquiry structure, Okanagan Treehouse's accommodation design, RCCV's community information, St James' design and donor storytelling, Upon This Rock's identity and Vertical Impression's explanation of a misunderstood medium. Cool Runnings' confirmed figure is a 30% increase in qualified bookings/jobs. Keep that meaning intact and use it where it earns its place.

Each article needs something an owner can use immediately: a page map, example form, scope comparison, content brief, training exercise or measurement worksheet. Concrete examples and clear recommendations do more work than another long introduction about the importance of websites.

## Local authority with a wider audience

Lead local service and industry articles with real operating detail: service coverage, office or site visits, seasonal updates, booking arrangements and what happens after the customer gets in touch. Platform and design articles can serve a wider audience without repeating city names in every heading. Link back to the relevant Okanagan service naturally.

Keep a limited number of useful provider comparisons alongside the broader library. The existing city comparisons continue to cover local shortlisting. The new branding comparison identifies Michael as its author and a listed provider; selection is based on project fit rather than an invented independent ranking.

## Internal links

Each article has one primary commercial destination, a small number of genuinely related resources and a final invitation to get in touch. Future resource links become active only when the destination is published. Industry advice links to its matching industry service page; platform/design/buying content generally links to web design; relevant operations content links to AI implementation; brand/video topics link to marketing and branding.

## How to judge whether this is working

Track useful search queries and the pages they reach, then enquiries, qualified conversations and accepted projects. A large library is an asset only when its answers remain useful and bring the right people closer to a decision. Keep improving the articles that show actual interest rather than expanding the page count for its own sake.

## Research scope

Each new topic has its own buyer-task query snapshot, five returned search results and at least one retrieved page used to examine coverage. Primary documentation, existing project sources and the earlier industry research supply the factual basis. These snapshots are not a personalized Google ranking report or search-volume estimate. Copied third-party source pages remain in a private, deployment-excluded research folder.

Echo by Fulcrum produced all 60 full drafts. A separate constrained Echo editorial pass completed for 57; three malformed API review responses were replaced with full source-led main-editor reviews. Main-editor fact corrections were applied across the library. Local checks validate structure, links, claim-sensitive wording, duplication and unpublished state. This does not constitute an external AI-detector score. The review reader distinguishes draft preparation from publication.
